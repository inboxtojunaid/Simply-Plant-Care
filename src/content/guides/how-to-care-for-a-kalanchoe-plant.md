A kalanchoe is a succulent that flowers for months at a time, and caring for it comes down to three things: plenty of bright light, watering only when the soil is dry, and — if you want it to flower again — giving it long, dark nights in autumn. Treat it like a succulent that happens to flower, not like a thirsty flowering plant, and it will thrive.

Kalanchoe is one of the most popular flowering houseplants because it combines the toughness of a succulent with months of small, bright flowers in red, pink, orange, yellow, or white. Its thick, glossy leaves store water, so it forgives a forgotten watering far more easily than most flowering plants. The main reason people give up on it is that it stops flowering — and that has a fix.

## Light: the brighter, the better

Kalanchoe needs more light than most houseplants. A sunny windowsill — south- or west-facing is ideal — suits it well, and it can take several hours of direct sun. In lower light it survives, but the stems stretch, the leaves grow sparse, and flowering is weak or absent. If your plant looks leggy with long gaps between leaves, it is asking for more light.

In summer, a kalanchoe can go outdoors onto a sunny patio or balcony, which often encourages a strong flush of growth. Bring it back inside before night temperatures drop below about 10°C, and move it gradually into full sun outdoors so the leaves do not scorch.

## Watering: succulent rules apply

Water a kalanchoe only when the soil has dried out through the pot. In practice that is roughly every one to two weeks in the growing season and even less in winter. Water thoroughly, let the excess drain away completely, and never let the pot sit in water.

Overwatering is the main way to kill a kalanchoe. Its thick leaves store water, so it copes with drought far better than with soggy soil. Signs of overwatering include soft, translucent lower leaves, a mushy stem base, and black spots on the leaves. If the soil is wet and the plant looks unwell, stop watering, let the soil dry fully, and check the drainage holes are not blocked.

## Soil and pot

Use a loose, fast-draining mix — a cactus or succulent potting mix is ideal, or ordinary potting mix with a generous handful of perlite or coarse grit mixed in. The pot must have drainage holes. Kalanchoe stays compact and prefers a snug pot, so repot only every two to three years, in spring, moving up one size.

## Feeding and deadheading

Feed once a month from spring to early autumn with a balanced liquid fertiliser at half strength. Do not feed in winter, and do not overfeed — too much fertiliser produces soft, leggy growth and few flowers.

Deadhead spent flower clusters by cutting the flower stalk back to just above a pair of leaves. Removing faded flowers keeps the plant tidy and encourages it to put energy into new growth rather than seed. Once all the flowers on a stem have finished, cut the whole stem back.

## Pruning leggy growth

After flowering, kalanchoe often grows tall and straggly. In spring, you can cut leggy stems back by up to half, just above a leaf joint, and the plant will branch out with fresh, bushy growth. The cuttings root easily in potting mix or water, so you can start new plants from the pieces you prune off.

## Common mistakes

- **Watering little and often.** Kalanchoe prefers a thorough soak followed by a proper dry-out. Frequent small waterings keep the top wet and the roots dry.
- **Keeping it in low light.** A kalanchoe in a dim room stretches, drops leaves, and will not flower. Give it your sunniest windowsill.
- **Leaving it in standing water.** The pot should drain fully after every watering; a full saucer is the fastest route to root rot.
- **Skipping the dark nights.** Without long, uninterrupted nights in autumn, a kalanchoe simply will not set buds — no matter how healthy it looks.
- **Feeding in winter.** The plant rests in winter. Feeding then produces weak growth and can damage the roots.

## Troubleshooting

**Leaves are soft and translucent at the base.** Overwatering. Let the soil dry out completely, remove any mushy leaves, and check the roots are not black and rotten. If the stem base is mushy, take a healthy cutting and start again.

**The plant is tall and leggy with few leaves.** Not enough light. Move it to a brighter spot and prune the leggy stems back in spring to encourage branching.

**It grows well but never flowers.** Almost always a light or day-length problem. First, give it more light. Then, in autumn, run the six-week short-day treatment described above.

**Brown, crispy leaf edges.** Usually sun scorch after a sudden move into strong sun, or salt build-up from over-fertilising. Move it into sun gradually, and flush the soil with plain water if you have been feeding heavily.

**White powdery patches on the leaves.** Powdery mildew, encouraged by poor air circulation and damp leaves. Remove affected leaves, improve airflow, and avoid wetting the foliage when watering.

## Frequently asked questions

### How often should I water a kalanchoe?

Only when the soil has dried out through the pot — roughly every one to two weeks in the growing season, less in winter. Water thoroughly and let it drain completely.

### Why won't my kalanchoe flower again?

Kalanchoe needs long, dark nights to set flower buds. In autumn, give it about 14 hours of uninterrupted darkness each night for six weeks. Low light can also prevent flowering, so make sure it has a bright spot during the day.

### Can a kalanchoe live outside?

In summer, yes — it enjoys a sunny patio or balcony once night temperatures stay above about 10°C. It is not frost-hardy, so it must come indoors before the cold arrives.

### Is kalanchoe toxic to pets?

Kalanchoe is considered toxic to cats and dogs if eaten, so keep it out of reach of pets that chew plants, and out of reach of small children.

### Should I cut off the dead flowers?

Yes. Cut each spent flower cluster back to just above a pair of leaves. This keeps the plant tidy and encourages new growth.

### How long do kalanchoe flowers last?

A single flush of flowers can last two to three months with good light and care, which is one reason kalanchoe is such a popular gift plant.

## Conclusion

Kalanchoe is an easy plant to keep alive and a satisfying one to rebloom once you know its secret. Give it a sunny spot, water it like the succulent it is, prune it back after flowering, and run the short-day treatment in autumn. Do that, and the same plant can flower for months, year after year.
