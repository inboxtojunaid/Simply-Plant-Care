A ficus tree — most often *Ficus benjamina*, the weeping fig — is a beautiful indoor tree with glossy leaves and an elegant branching shape. It is also famous for one thing: dropping its leaves the moment anything changes. Move it, water it differently, let a cold draught in, and leaves fall. The good news is that leaf drop is usually a complaint, not a death sentence, and a ficus that is left in one good spot with steady care will settle down and grow well for years.

The secret to ficus care is consistency. Pick the brightest spot you can offer, water it on a steady rhythm, and then leave it alone. Ficus trees dislike being moved, repotted often, or fussed over.

## Light and position

A ficus wants bright, indirect light. A spot near a south- or west-facing window, set back a little from the glass, or near a bright east-facing window, suits it well. A few hours of gentle morning sun is fine; strong midday sun through glass can scorch the leaves.

Choose the spot carefully and leave the tree there: every move can trigger leaf drop while it adjusts. A quarter turn every couple of weeks is fine; shifting it across the room weekly is not.

Low light is the most common reason a ficus slowly thins out: leaves drop from the lower and inner branches and growth becomes sparse. If that is happening, move the tree closer to the window gradually, over a week.

## Watering

Ficus trees like their soil lightly moist, not wet and not dry. The rhythm matters more than the amount on any single day.

1. **Check the top 3–5 cm of soil.** Water when it feels dry.
2. **Water thoroughly** until it runs out of the drainage hole.
3. **Empty the saucer** after 15 minutes. A ficus in standing water will drop leaves fast.
4. **Keep the rhythm steady.** In spring and summer that may mean weekly watering; in winter, every two weeks or less.

Both overwatering and underwatering cause leaf drop, which is why the soil check matters: wet soil plus falling yellow leaves means too much water; dry soil plus falling green leaves means too little. Change one thing at a time and give the tree a few weeks to respond.

## Humidity, temperature and draughts

Ficus trees prefer steady warmth and moderate humidity. Cold draughts — from a frequently opened front door, a draughty window or an air-conditioning vent — are a classic cause of sudden leaf drop. Radiators are the opposite problem: hot, dry air that dries leaf edges and encourages spider mites.

If your home is very dry in winter, group the ficus with other plants, stand it on a pebble tray, or move it to a more humid room. A light misting helps the leaves briefly but does not change the room's humidity for long, so do not rely on it alone.

## Soil, pot and feeding

Ordinary houseplant potting mix with a handful of perlite is ideal. The pot must have drainage holes. Repot only when the tree is clearly root-bound — usually every two to three years in spring — and go up just one pot size. Ficus trees resent unnecessary repotting, and a repot is one of the most reliable triggers for a leaf-drop episode, so do it only when needed and keep conditions steady afterwards.

Feed with a balanced liquid fertiliser at half strength once a month in spring and summer. Do not feed in autumn and winter, and do not overfeed to try to force growth after leaf drop — that usually makes things worse.

## Pruning

Prune in late winter or early spring, before growth restarts. Remove dead or crossing branches, shorten overly long shoots to keep the tree's shape, and thin the centre lightly so light reaches the inner leaves. Ficus sap is milky and can irritate skin, so wear gloves and wipe the secateurs clean afterwards. Do not remove more than about a third of the canopy in one year. Pruning after a leaf-drop episode, once the tree has stabilised, encourages bushy regrowth.

## Common mistakes

- **Moving it constantly.** Every move can trigger leaf drop. Find its spot and leave it there.
- **Watering on a strict schedule.** Seasons change, and so does the tree's water use. Check the soil every time.
- **Placing it by a door or radiator.** Cold draughts and hot dry air are the two worst neighbours for a ficus.
- **Repotting too often.** A ficus does not need a new pot every year, and each repot risks a leaf-drop episode.
- **Panicking after leaf drop.** Shedding leaves after a change is normal. Fix the cause, keep conditions steady, and wait — new growth usually follows within weeks.

## Troubleshooting

### My ficus is dropping lots of leaves at once

Use the checklist above. In most cases the cause is a recent change: a move, a light change, a draught, or a watering change. Correct the cause, keep everything else steady, and be patient. A ficus that keeps its branches supple and shows small green buds will usually regrow.

### Leaves are yellowing before they fall

Yellow leaves that fall usually point to overwatering or to natural shedding of the oldest leaves. If many leaves yellow at once, check the soil: if it is wet, let it dry out and check the drainage; if it is dry, water thoroughly.

### Leaves are dry and crispy at the edges

Dry air or underwatering. Check the soil first, then look at the tree's neighbours — a radiator or a hot, sunny window can dry the leaves even when the soil is moist. Improve humidity and move the tree away from heat sources.

### The tree is bare at the bottom and leafy at the top

This is common in low light or after years without pruning. Move the tree to a brighter spot and prune back the top growth in spring to encourage lower branches to leaf out.

### Sticky leaves or tiny bumps on the stems

Check for scale insects — small brown bumps on stems and leaf undersides — which leave sticky honeydew. Wipe them off by hand, wash the foliage, and check nearby plants. Spider mites leave fine webbing and speckled leaves; a lukewarm shower and higher humidity help.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why is my ficus tree dropping leaves?

Ficus trees drop leaves when something changes — a move, a change in light, a cold draught, a watering change, or repotting. Work through the checklist above, fix the most likely cause, keep conditions steady, and give it several weeks to settle.

### How often should I water a ficus tree?

Water when the top 3–5 cm of soil feels dry, soaking thoroughly and letting the excess drain away. That is roughly weekly in summer and every two weeks or less in winter, depending on light and temperature.

### Can a ficus tree recover after losing most of its leaves?

Often, yes. If the branches are still flexible and there are small green buds, the tree is alive. Keep it in bright indirect light, water correctly, avoid moving it, and new leaves usually appear within a few weeks in the growing season.

### Does a ficus like direct sunlight?

A little gentle morning sun is fine, but strong midday sun through glass can scorch the leaves. Bright, indirect light is the safest choice indoors.

### When should I repot my ficus?

Every two to three years in spring, or when roots are circling the pot and growth has stalled. Go up one pot size, use fresh mix, and expect some leaf drop afterwards — keep conditions steady while it settles.

### Is ficus safe for pets?

Ficus benjamina is toxic to cats and dogs if eaten, and its milky sap can irritate skin. Keep it out of reach of pets that chew plants and wear gloves when pruning.

## Conclusion

Caring for a ficus tree is mostly about not disturbing it. Give it bright indirect light, water it when the top of the soil dries, keep it away from draughts and radiators, and leave it in one place. It will complain about change by dropping leaves — and then, if you hold steady, it will quietly grow them back.
