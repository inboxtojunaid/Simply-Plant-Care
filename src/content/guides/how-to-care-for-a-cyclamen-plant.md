A cyclamen is a plant with a calendar. It wakes in autumn, flowers all winter, fades in spring, sleeps through summer, and wakes again — if you let it. Most cyclamen deaths happen because someone treats it like an ordinary houseplant: warm room, water from above, bin it when the leaves go yellow. Every one of those is exactly wrong. The plant isn't difficult. It's just on a different schedule from yours.

The ones sold as houseplants are usually florist's cyclamen, with swept-back petals in pink, red, white, or purple above a mound of heart-shaped, silver-marbled leaves. They turn up as winter gifts, and many are thrown away when the flowers fade. With the right care they come back and flower again the following year. Here's the year, season by season.

## Autumn: the season it wakes up

Autumn is when the story restarts. Small new leaves appear at the crown of the tuber, and flower buds usually follow within weeks.

This is the moment to move the pot back to a bright, cool windowsill and resume regular watering from the base. It's also the moment to repot, if the tuber is crowded. Cyclamen like a snug pot, so only move up one size, and use fresh, well-draining potting mix. Set the tuber so its top sits just at the soil surface — burying it invites rot, and leaving it too high up dries it out. Water the plant in from the base and put it back in its cool, bright spot.

Light in autumn is straightforward: bright and indirect. A little gentle morning sun is fine, but strong direct sun heats the leaves and dries the soil too quickly.

## Winter: flowers for months, if you keep it cool

Winter is the payoff. A healthy cyclamen in a cool room keeps producing new buds for two to three months, sometimes longer. A single flower lasts a week or two, but the plant keeps replacing them.

The number that matters here is temperature: 10–18 °C. An east- or north-facing windowsill, a cool hallway, or an unheated room that stays in that range is ideal. Warm rooms are the main reason cyclamen fail indoors. Above 20 °C the flowers fade fast, the leaves yellow, and the plant collapses into an early dormancy. Keep it away from radiators and hot-air vents — the coolest bright spot you have is the right one.

While it's flowering, keep the soil lightly moist but not soggy, and feed every two weeks with a balanced liquid fertiliser at half the label strength. Deadhead spent flowers regularly — and do it the cyclamen way. Instead of snipping, grasp the faded flower stalk near its base and give it a firm twist and pull. The whole stalk should come away cleanly from the tuber. Do the same with any leaves that have yellowed completely. Removing spent flowers and yellowing leaves this way keeps the plant tidy and reduces the risk of rot.

## Spring: the fade is not the end

In late spring the flowers become fewer and the leaves start to yellow from the edges. This is the moment most people give up on the plant. Don't.

It's going dormant, and the rest is a normal part of its life. Gradually reduce watering and stop feeding. Remove yellow leaves and spent flower stalks as they loosen. The timeline below follows each phase of the cycle, so you can see where your plant is and what it needs next.

## Summer: the rest, and the tuber that waits

By summer, most or all of the leaves have gone. The pot looks empty. It isn't dead — the tuber is resting underground, and how you treat it now decides whether it returns in autumn.

Move the pot to a cool, shaded spot. A garage shelf or a shaded outdoor corner works. Water only a tiny amount every few weeks, just enough to stop the tuber shrivelling. Don't feed it at all. And whatever you do, don't throw it away. A dormant tuber stored cool and nearly dry will usually regrow in autumn.

The one thing that kills a dormant tuber is water. A saucer full of standing water suffocates the roots and rots it. If the tuber goes soft and mushy, that's rot, usually from overwatering during dormancy. A firm tuber that hasn't sprouted by mid-autumn may simply be late — keep it cool and lightly watered for a few more weeks before giving up.

## Watering from below, all year

If there's one technique to take away from this guide, it's this one. The crown of a cyclamen — the swollen tuber at the soil surface where the leaves and flower stems emerge — rots easily if it stays wet. So always water from the base.

Set the pot in a saucer or bowl of water for ten to fifteen minutes, let the soil soak it up, then remove the pot and let it drain fully. Never leave the pot standing in water. While the plant is in flower, water when the surface feels dry to the touch, which in a cool room may be once a week or less. In summer, watering drops to almost nothing.

Watering from above is the classic cyclamen mistake, and it rots the tuber from the top down. Water on the crown, standing water in the saucer, a warm room — that's the combination that kills them. Get the watering right, keep it cool, and the rest of the year mostly takes care of itself.

## Where it usually goes wrong

**Flowers droop and leaves go limp.** Usually the plant is too warm or too dry. Move it somewhere cooler and soak the pot from the base for fifteen minutes. A cyclamen often perks up within hours.

**Leaves yellow while it's still flowering.** A few yellowing older leaves are normal, but widespread yellowing in a warm room means heat stress. Move the plant to a cooler spot and check that the soil is not waterlogged.

**Flowers fade within days of bringing it home.** A warm, dry room and a plant that was grown cool in a nursery is a harsh transition. Give it the coolest bright spot you have, water from the base, and the next flush of buds should last longer.

**No regrowth after summer dormancy.** Check the tuber: firm is good, soft and mushy means rot. A firm tuber that hasn't sprouted by mid-autumn may just be late.

**Grey mould on leaves or stalks.** Grey mould thrives in damp, still air. Remove affected leaves, improve air circulation, avoid wetting the foliage, and water from the base only.

## Quick answers

### How long do cyclamen flowers last?

A single flower lasts a week or two, but a healthy plant in a cool room keeps producing new buds for two to three months, sometimes longer. Warmth shortens flowering dramatically.

### Is my cyclamen dead when the leaves turn yellow in spring?

Almost certainly not. Cyclamen naturally go dormant in late spring and summer. The leaves yellow and die back while the tuber rests. Keep the pot cool and nearly dry, and look for new leaves in early autumn.

### Can cyclamen grow outdoors?

In mild climates with sheltered, well-drained soil, hardy cyclamen types can grow outdoors, but the florist's cyclamen sold as a houseplant is not frost-hardy. Indoors, a cool windowsill is its best home.

### Why is my cyclamen drooping?

Drooping is usually a sign of heat or dry soil. Move the plant to a cooler spot and soak the pot from the base. If the soil is wet and the plant still droops, the tuber may be rotting — check that the pot drains and the crown is not sitting wet.

### How often should I water a cyclamen?

While flowering, water from the base whenever the soil surface feels dry — roughly once a week in a cool room. During summer dormancy, reduce watering to a tiny amount every few weeks.

### Do cyclamen come back every year?

Yes, with the right dormancy care. A healthy tuber can flower again each autumn and winter for several years. Keep it cool, let it rest in summer, and repot in early autumn when new growth starts.
