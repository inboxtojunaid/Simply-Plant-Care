A cyclamen needs a cool room, bright indirect light, and watering from the base of the pot — never over the crown of the plant. Get those three things right and it will flower for weeks or even months, far longer than most flowering houseplants. The trick is understanding that a cyclamen is not a typical tropical plant: it likes it cool, and it expects a summer rest.

Cyclamen sold as houseplants are usually florist's cyclamen, with swept-back petals in pink, red, white, or purple above a mound of heart-shaped, silver-marbled leaves. They are often bought as a winter gift plant, and many are thrown away when the flowers fade — but with the right care they can come back and flower again the following year.

## Light and temperature: cool is key

Cyclamen flower best in a cool, bright spot — an east- or north-facing windowsill, a cool hallway, or an unheated room that stays between about 10 and 18°C. Warm rooms are the main reason cyclamen fail indoors: above 20°C the flowers fade fast, the leaves yellow, and the plant collapses into an early dormancy.

Give it bright, indirect light. A little gentle morning sun is fine, but strong direct sun heats the leaves and dries the soil too quickly. In winter, when the light is weak, the brightest windowsill you have is usually the right place — just keep it away from radiators and hot-air vents.

## Watering: from the base, never the crown

The crown of a cyclamen — the swollen tuber at the soil surface where the leaves and flower stems emerge — rots easily if it stays wet. So always water from the base: set the pot in a saucer or bowl of water for ten to fifteen minutes, let the soil soak it up, then remove the pot and let it drain fully. Never leave the pot standing in water.

While the plant is in flower, keep the soil lightly moist but not soggy. Water when the surface feels dry to the touch, which in a cool room may be once a week or less. In summer, when the plant is dormant, watering drops to almost nothing — more on that in the dormancy section below.

## Feeding and deadheading

Feed every two weeks while the plant is flowering, using a balanced liquid fertiliser at half the label strength. Stop feeding once the flowers finish and the leaves begin to yellow.

Deadhead spent flowers regularly. Instead of snipping, grasp the faded flower stalk near its base and give it a firm twist and pull — the whole stalk should come away cleanly from the tuber. Removing spent flowers and yellowing leaves this way keeps the plant tidy and reduces the risk of rot. Do the same with any leaves that have yellowed completely.

## Repotting

Repot in early autumn, just as new growth appears. Cyclamen like a snug pot, so only move up one size, and use fresh, well-draining potting mix. Set the tuber so its top sits just at the soil surface — burying it invites rot, and leaving it too high up dries it out. Water the plant in from the base and place it back in its cool, bright spot.

## Common mistakes

- **Keeping it in a warm living room.** Heat is the number one cyclamen killer indoors. A cool windowsill or hallway is far better than a cosy spot by the radiator.
- **Watering from above.** Water on the crown rots the tuber. Always water from the base and let the pot drain.
- **Throwing it away when it goes dormant.** Yellowing leaves in late spring are a rest period, not death. A dormant tuber stored cool and dry will usually regrow in autumn.
- **Letting it sit in water.** A saucer full of standing water suffocates the roots and rots the tuber. Always empty the saucer after soaking.
- **Burying the tuber when repotting.** The top of the tuber should sit at the soil surface.

## Troubleshooting

**Flowers droop and leaves go limp.** Usually the plant is too warm or too dry. Move it somewhere cooler and soak the pot from the base for fifteen minutes. A cyclamen often perks up within hours.

**Leaves yellow while it is still flowering.** A few yellowing older leaves are normal, but widespread yellowing in a warm room means heat stress. Move the plant to a cooler spot and check that the soil is not waterlogged.

**Flowers fade within days of bringing it home.** A warm, dry room and a plant that was grown cool in a nursery is a harsh transition. Give it the coolest bright spot you have, water from the base, and the next flush of buds should last longer.

**No regrowth after summer dormancy.** Check the tuber: firm is good, soft and mushy means rot, usually from overwatering during dormancy. A firm tuber that has not sprouted by mid-autumn may simply be late — keep it cool and lightly watered for a few more weeks before giving up.

**Grey mould on leaves or stalks.** Grey mould thrives in damp, still air. Remove affected leaves, improve air circulation, avoid wetting the foliage, and water from the base only.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long do cyclamen flowers last?

A single flower lasts a week or two, but a healthy plant in a cool room keeps producing new buds for two to three months, sometimes longer. Warmth shortens flowering dramatically.

### Is my cyclamen dead when the leaves turn yellow in spring?

Almost certainly not. Cyclamen naturally go dormant in late spring and summer. The leaves yellow and die back while the tuber rests. Keep the pot cool and nearly dry, and look for new leaves in early autumn.

### Can cyclamen grow outdoors?

In mild climates with sheltered, well-drained soil, hardy cyclamen types can grow outdoors, but the florist's cyclamen sold as a houseplant is not frost-hardy. Indoors, a cool windowsill is its best home.

### Why is my cyclamen drooping?

Drooping is usually a sign of heat or dry soil. Move the plant to a cooler spot and soak the pot from the base. If the soil is wet and the plant still droops, the tuber may be rotting — check that the pot drains and the crown is not sitting wet.

### How often should I water a cyclamen?

While flowering, water from the base whenever the soil surface feels dry — roughly once a week in a cool room. During summer dormancy, reduce watering to a tiny amount every few weeks.

### Do cyclamen come back every year?

Yes, with the right dormancy care. A healthy tuber can flower again each autumn and winter for several years. Keep it cool, let it rest in summer, and repot in early autumn when new growth starts.

## Conclusion

Cyclamen reward a cool room and a little patience. Water from the base, keep it out of the heat, deadhead the spent flowers, and — most importantly — let it rest in summer instead of throwing it away when the leaves fade. Do that, and the same plant will come back and flower again next autumn.
